VERDICT: WORTH RUNNING. Every check passed.

Warnings (6) - not disqualifying, but say them in the paper:
  1. 49% of moved objects do not return to where they were - the reversion measurement is mostly measuring the world, not the memory
  2. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' drops 9.3 points at the return on the objects the upset never touched, against 5.5 on the ones it moved - most of the fourth leg is the return being a harder prediction problem, not a memory losing a routine; do not read it as the latter
  3. settled level (three-day memory) passes with under 2 points to spare, so that leg is decided by noise rather than by the scenario; do not quote it as a margin
  4. the verdict is one class deep: dropping 'towel' or 'glass' or 'water_bottle' on its own reverses it - the discriminating leg(s) break (three-day memory) fail without them, which means the disruption itself rests on one class; the floor(s) learn (three-day memory), settled level (three-day memory) fail without them, which is fragility rather than a fitted disruption. If this scenario's class list was chosen by trying mixes against this gate then the gate is not an independent test of it - say in the paper which scenarios predate the gate and which were tuned against it
  5. this verdict is not robust to the thresholds: moving every bar together by 2 points reverses it at +2 (stricter). The bars were set from what one scenario and two older ones showed, so a verdict that turns on them is the gate's opinion rather than the scenario's - do not quote it without this caveat
  6. 'a timetable that never forgets' break again is +0.4 points with a household-clustered error of 2.5, inside two standard errors of nothing; this null only excludes a change larger than about 5 points, it does not show there is none
