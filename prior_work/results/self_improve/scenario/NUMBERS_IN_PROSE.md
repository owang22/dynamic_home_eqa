## THE TRAP THAT CAUGHT US THREE TIMES: pooled share vs per-object accuracy

Three separate numbers in this work were wrong in the same way, so this is a
systematic trap rather than a slip. **A pooled modal share and a per-object accuracy
are different quantities and they can point in opposite directions.**

- *pooled modal share*: of all questions about class C across ten households, the
  fraction answered by the single commonest receptacle. Low when households differ,
  even if every object is perfectly predictable. `counts.py`-style tables report this.
- *per-object accuracy*: for each object, the fraction of its questions answered by
  its OWN commonest place, averaged over objects. This is the one that means "a
  settled belief about this thing is weak or strong".

What it cost: the laptop in wfh_v1 reads **51% pooled** and **100% per object**, and
the wrong one was quoted as evidence that the robot held a weak belief about it -
close to the opposite of the truth, and it was repeated onward before being caught.
The towel (88% pooled, 89-94% per object) and glasses (25% pooled) were the same
error. **Every per-object accuracy anywhere needs testing against this distinction
before it is quoted.** Any line below that reads "right N% of the time" is a
per-object claim and must have been computed per object.
