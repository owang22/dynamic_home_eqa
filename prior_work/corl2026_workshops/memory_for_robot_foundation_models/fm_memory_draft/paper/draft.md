# Memory that forgets on cue: what a home robot's memory must do when the household's routine shifts

*Draft for the CoRL 2026 workshop "Memory for Robot Foundation Models" (4 pages excl. references). All runs final, 2026-09-22; figures and tables regenerate from `results/fm_memory` via `tools/figs.py`, `tools/paired.py`, `tools/endmsg_contrast.py`, `tools/owner_paired.py`.*

## Abstract

A home robot that answers "where is my mug?" from what it has seen must remember the household's routine, not the last frame. We ask what that memory should store and how it gets updated when the routine shifts, on a symbolic household simulator with a scripted shift: 14 days of an ordinary routine, ten days with one resident off sick (their everyday things migrate from the desk to the couch), eight days back at work. Questions are about that resident's things while they are in use, and we score the *cold* questions — the first about an object each day, before any feedback — where recency is worthless (last-seen: 14%) and only a memory of the routine can answer. We compare two LLM memories practitioners deploy, a replay buffer of dated sightings and a nightly consolidated routine table, against counters that never forget, forget with a half-life, or index memory by regime. The buffer learns the routine (66% cold, against 73% for a counter) and collapses at the shift like everything else, but then does not re-learn from ten days of feedback: it sits 37.5 points below the forgetting counter inside the spell (se 3.8) though the two are level in the lead-up (−4.6, se 3.3, not detected). A replay buffer is memory without an update rule. The consolidated table never learns the routine at all (40% cold); rewritten nightly, it keeps the modal day and drops the in-use windows the questions are about. One sentence — "she is home sick today" — is what updates the buffer: +33.3 cold points inside the spell (se 3.7, 18 households), concentrated on the objects whose new placement world knowledge can supply, and on a second bank set it moves the sick resident's things (+15.3, se 3.8) while leaving the other resident's untouched (+0.4, se 2.1). Left standing after the spell, the same sentence costs 7 points on all questions and 12 on cold ones in the first three days back and nothing thereafter; a retraction leaves no detectable damage, but cannot itself be shown to be what repairs it (+8.9 cold, se 4.8, not detected over 18 households). The buffer's stated confidence is 0.87 in every stage. A counter that stores each sighting under the regime it was seen in re-learns as fast as the forgetting counter with a return drop a fifth the size (−4.7 against −22.9): the shift should be a retrieval key, not a caption and not a forgetting trigger.

## 1. Setup: a routine shift that recency cannot answer

- Simulator, household, resident cards; the walkthrough; the observation channel: one patrol round at 03:00 + found-it feedback 10 min after each question (an ordinary sighting every agent receives; no active sensing).
- Calendar: days 0-13 lead (weekday routine), 14-23 resident_1 off sick (placement rules: book / tablet / mug / water bottle / charger / glasses / glass / razor go to the couch and coffee table), 24-31 return. 10 households (seeds 0-9), 12-16 questions/day, one question per object per 30 min at most.
- Question type: the sick resident's everyday things, asked *during* the activity that uses them. Why this and not "where is the mug at 08:00": a timing shift does not change the answer to a use-time question, only a placement shift does; and static objects are 90-100% for every agent (dropped). Regime novelty = share of sick-stage questions whose truth differs from the lead answer for that (object, 2 h bin): 51% against a 21% same-regime baseline — the ceiling of any drop.
- **Households.** 10 on the main arms (seeds 0-9); 18 on the message contrasts (two of the twenty had a day with nothing stable to ask about and were dropped before any run: a tidying mood plus a restless evening on the same day leaves every target object in someone's hands at every candidate moment, so the bank builder finds no question with a stable in-house truth); 10 for everything else.
- **How every contrast is stated** (convention shared with the companion study, revised 2026-09-22): the mean of *paired per-household differences* with its **standard error** and n; an effect is called **detected** when it is at least twice its standard error, and is then described as small or large on its own merits. The household-to-household **spread (sd)** is reported beside it as heterogeneity, never as the test. Below n=6 the standard error is not trustworthy, so there the stricter bar applies instead — the effect must exceed the spread — and the rule in force is named. We also report which claims pass the stricter bar (effect larger than the between-household spread), because several pass one bar and not the other: that bar was our original criterion and it is what caught a three-household claim of ours that did not replicate.
- Controls that rule out shortcuts: last-seen is flat (48 | 57 | 51); the frozen timetable shows what "never forget" does (74 | 64 | 81); the 3-day-half-life timetable shows what "forget" does (74 | 78 | 70). Every LLM arm sees the identical stream; prompts are checked for hidden state before they are sent; temperature 0.

## 2. Three memories for the same stream

1. **Replay buffer** (`naive`): the object's newest 60 sightings (dated), plus the spots checked empty since its last sighting; the LLM answers one spot + a confidence from that list. Nothing is ever written; retrieval is by object.
2. **Nightly routine table** (`routine`): once per night the LLM rewrites a per-object table ("mug: cupboard 00:00-09:35; kitchen table 09:36-11:25; desk 11:26-21:04; coffee table 21:05-") from the previous table and the day's sightings of every object that has ever moved; questions are answered from the table plus the object's last 3 sightings. This is the "reflection" / semantic-memory design; the free-form-notes variant of the same idea (`summary`, at most 350 words on every object) is reported as an ablation — it saturates its token budget on static objects and never learns (58 | 62 | 54 cold, 3 households).
3. **Regime-tagged counter** (`tagged`): the 2-hour-bin timetable, but every sighting on a day the resident said "I am home sick" goes into that regime's own bins; lead bins are never overwritten. A 20-line change to the counter; the LLM analogue is the same table with a "since day 14, while she is home sick" column (not run: the counter version is the argument).

Message arms (naming shared with the companion study): **no message** / **start message** (one dated line per sick day, "Day 14: Yuki is home sick today.") / **start + end messages** (also "Day 24: Yuki is feeling better and back at work today."). Model: Qwen3.8-27B, local vLLM, temperature 0, JSON answers.

## 3. Results (all arms final; 10 households on the main arms, 18 on the message contrasts, 6 on the partial shift)

Two views of every number: **all questions**, and **cold questions** — the first question about an object on a given day, before any found-it feedback for it (a third of all questions). Warm questions are answered by recency at ~80% by every agent including last-seen; cold questions are where a memory of the routine is needed (last-seen: 14 | 6 | 15).

- **Learning.** The buffer LLM learns the routine from the stream: cold accuracy climbs 28 → 88 over the lead (day 12), close to the 2-hour-bin counter (12 → 98). It reaches this by reading a dated list of 60 sightings; it is the *only* LLM memory that does.
- **Break.** Every learner collapses on sick day 1 (counters 31, buffer 23, cold). The tagged counter falls furthest (11: all its sick bins are empty and it says so — confidence 0.42 vs 0.54).
- **Inside the spell the buffer does not re-learn.** Cold accuracy stays at 19-49 (mean 35) for ten days while every counter climbs back to 65-94 from the same feedback: paired, the buffer is **37.5 points below** the half-life counter in the spell (se 3.8, 9.8 se, and larger than the spread; all questions 13.5, se 1.6) though the two are level in the lead (−4.6, se 3.3, not detected). Its own late-spell-minus-lead change is −24.7 cold (se 5.6, 4.4 se) where the counter's is +12.7 (se 3.9). The model reads the lead-regime sightings as the routine ("consistently found at desk_b1 during work hours (day 8, day 10, day 12)" — on sick day 5) and adapts only within a day, by recency: on all questions it is at 64 in the spell, on cold ones 35. A replay buffer is memory without an update rule; the LLM's implicit weighting of 60 dated sightings is "the majority wins," which is the frozen counter's rule — the frozen counter at least gets 60.
- **Two verdicts changed when the rule changed, not when the data did.** The buffer's no-message return-day *rise* (+18.2 cold, se 6.5) and the half-life counter's cold return drop (−15.0, se 6.7) were reported as "no measurable difference" under our earlier bar (the effect must exceed the between-household spread) and are detected at two standard errors. The new bar is the *more permissive* of the two — for six or more households, exceeding the spread already implies exceeding two standard errors, since the spread is √n standard errors — so nothing published under the old rule loses its standing and the change can only add claims. Both of these are below the spread: real in sign, variable in size across homes.
- **Return.** Paired within-agent change from the late spell to return days 24-26, all questions: the half-life counter drops **−22.9, se 4.3** (5.3 se, and larger than the spread) while the regime-tagged counter drops **−4.7, se 1.3** — detected, but a fifth the size; the frozen counter (+8.3, se 6.9) and the untold buffer (+5.6, se 5.1) show no detected change. On cold questions the half-life counter's drop is −15.0, se 6.7 (2.2 se, detected but below the spread).
- **Consolidation loses the routine.** All three consolidated memories plateau at 40-58 cold in the lead and 19-22 in the spell. The nightly table is rewritten as the *latest* day's timeline even when seven days of timelines are in the prompt: after day 1 the mug's line reads "cupboard 03:00-09:36; kitchen table 09:36-11:26; desk 11:26-21:05; coffee table 21:05-", after day 3 "pantry 03:00-09:38; desk 09:38-23:59" — the breakfast and evening-TV windows, which are what the questions ask about, are gone. A "typical day" narrative compresses to the modal story; the sparse in-use windows are exactly what it drops.
- **Confidence, calibrated.** Removing the level offset with a monotone map fit on the lead days only (last-5-lead convention; table from the study's shared extractor), the buffer's confidence-minus-accuracy gap is −1.5 in the lead, **+15.2** on sick days 14-16 and +7.6 for the rest of the spell, back to +0.7 on the return: it is most overconfident exactly when its memory is stale, and never says so. With the start message the gap is +0.3 at the shift and −7.0 for the rest of the spell (more accurate than it admits once it has re-read the sightings), then **+10.1** on return days 24-26 (+5.3 with the end message as well). The routine table is milder (+3 / +5 / +1 / +3 / +1) because it sits lower to begin with.
- **Confidence discriminates within a stage but does not move between them.** Per arm the stated confidence has sd 0.10-0.14 over 6-10 distinct values spanning 0.20-0.98, and it ranks answers usefully: accuracy above an arm's median confidence is 21-42 points higher than below it. What never moves is the stage *mean* — the model lowers its confidence on the questions it is unsure of, but does not lower the whole distribution when the regime changes under it. That is the failure: not an inability to express uncertainty, but no signal that the world has changed.
- **Confidence, raw.** The buffer's stated confidence is 0.87 in every stage (accuracy 74 / 64 / 76; ECE 0.12 → 0.23 in the spell); the routine table's is 0.86 throughout at 61 / 62 / 59. The tagged counter's confidence follows its coverage: 0.54 → 0.50 → 0.68, lowest exactly when its bins are empty. Memory that cannot tell it is stale (it discriminates within a stage, but the whole distribution stays put across the shift) versus memory whose confidence *is* its coverage.

### 3.2 What an instruction updates (Fig. 2, Table 2)

- **Entry.** One dated sentence per sick day ("Day 14: Yuki is home sick today.") doubles the buffer's cold accuracy inside the spell, 35 → 68 — the level of the regime-tagged counter (67) — and lifts all-questions accuracy from 64 to 79. Paired: **+23.5 (se 4.2)** on days 14-16 and **+36.3 (se 5.9)** on days 17-23, 5.6 and 6.1 se, both also larger than the spread; all questions +12.9 (se 3.0) and +16.3 (se 3.6). Tested again on eight households built after the fact, the spell effect replicates at **+29.5 cold (se 4.0)** against the original +36.3 — pooled over all 18, **+33.3, se 3.7** (8.9 se). The same sightings, re-read under a different hypothesis. The routine table's gain is **not detected** (+12.1 cold, se 6.5, 1.9 se; +4.8 all, se 2.7): there is less in its memory to re-read.
- **Which things.** Per class, sick days 14-18, start message − no message (buffer): water bottle +24, mug +20, tablet +19, charger +16, glasses +13, book +6, glass 0, razor 0. The message helps where world knowledge fills in a new placement (the desk things move to the couch) and not where the routine is unchanged by illness (the glass at meals, the razor in the bathroom).
- **Return.** **Return.** With only the start message, nobody tells the robot the spell is over. Tested on **18 households** (the original ten plus eight built afterwards for this question), the message costs **7.0 points on all questions (se 2.2) and 12.3 on cold ones (se 3.9)** in the first three days back — detected at 3.1 and 3.2 se — and is gone by days 27-31 (−2.8, se 1.6; −4.2, se 2.7). That is **half what the first ten households showed** (−11.3 and −20.4): those ten were the optimistic sample, and the eight fresh ones give −1.6 and −2.2 on their own. Both figures are below the between-household spread (sd 9.5 and 16.5), so the effect is real and consistent in sign but varies a lot from home to home.

The asymmetry beside it is the more interesting result: **the entry effect replicates strongly on the fresh households (+29.5 cold, se 4.0, against the original +36.3) while the return cost does not.** Telling a robot about a change while the change is ongoing is a large and robust intervention; failing to retract it afterwards is a real but smaller and short-lived cost. Large and robust going in, real but smaller coming out.

Adding the end message ("Day 24: Yuki is feeling better and back at work today.") does **not** measurably repair it: start+end minus start-only on return days 24-26 is **+8.9 cold (se 4.8, 1.9 se)** and **+4.8 all (se 2.9, 1.6 se)**, neither detected, and by days 27-31 +2.7 and +0.5. Here too the sample tells a story: the first ten households gave +13.4, the eight new ones +3.4. On all questions a repair larger than about ten points is excluded; on cold questions anything up to about eighteen remains possible. We looked with eighteen households and did not find it, which is not the same as ruling it out. Against the no-message arm, the start+end arm's return-day damage is not detected at all (−2.2 all, se 2.2).

The mechanism the transcripts show is **"told but not yet believed"**: the arm carries the day-24 message from that day's first question and still answers from sick-regime sightings until the found-it feedback corroborates it, which takes most of the first three return days. An instruction that contradicts the memory's own recent evidence does not take effect until the evidence catches up — the same reason the entry message worked, where the sightings were merely ambiguous rather than contradictory.

- The counters are unaffected by any message by construction; the tagged counter *is* the message, used as an index.

### 3.2b Is the revision applied to the right beliefs? (partial shift: one resident sick, questions about everyone's things; 6 households)

Yes while the message is true, with a small leak once it is stale. Buffer memory, paired per household; levels are the mean of per-household values, every household weighted equally, over households with at least ten answers in that cell (count shown), which is the same weighting as the contrasts, so a level can never move one way while its contrast moves the other. All questions:

| window | the sick person's things | the other resident's things |
|---|---|---|
| sick 14-16 | 58 → 73, **+15.0 (se 4.3, 3.5 se)** detected, 6 hh | 72 → 73, +1.0 (se 2.8), 5 hh, not measurable |
| sick 17-23 | 70 → 86, **+15.3 (se 3.8, 4.0 se)** detected, 6 hh | 67 → 68, +0.4 (se 2.1), 6 hh, not detected |
| return 24-26 | 79 → 74, −5.2 (se 3.1), 5 hh, not measurable | 72 → 68, **−3.9 (se 1.6)**, 5 hh, clears the spread |
| return 27-31 | 75 → 74, −1.2 (se 1.3), 6 hh, not detected | 74 → 72, −1.9 (se 2.4), 6 hh, not detected |

Cold questions, the spell cells (the only ones with six households either side): the sick person's things +21.8 (se 3.6) and **+31.9 (se 3.5)**, the other resident's +0.7 (se 2.4) — near zero with a small standard error, which is half the claim. The routine table shows the same pattern much weaker: the sick person's things +6.8 (se 1.9) and +5.9 (se 2.7) on all questions, nothing detected on cold ones or on the other resident's things anywhere.

So the message is applied to the right things and does not bleed onto the rest *while the spell is running*; once the spell is over and the message still stands, the other resident's things are 3.9 points worse (five households, clearing the between-household spread under the small-n rule) where the sick person's own are not measurably changed. Selective revision is a property of an instruction that still matches the evidence, not of the memory's indexing — which is the same finding as the return cost on the one-person banks, seen from the other side.

### 3.3 Shift as retrieval key (Fig. 1, green)

Each sighting stored under the regime it was seen in; retrieval by (regime, time-of-day); lead bins never overwritten. Cold: 73 | 67 | 82; re-learns inside the spell as fast as the half-life counter (paired difference in the spell −5.8 ± 10.1 cold, no measurable difference) and its return drop is a fifth of the half-life counter's (−4.7 ± 4.3 vs −22.9 ± 13.6, all questions). The plasticity/stability trade-off is a consequence of a memory indexed by time only; indexed by context, both sides are had at once. The LLM analogue is one more column in the routine table — or, for the buffer, the message used to *partition* the sightings rather than to caption them.

## 4. What this says about memory for robot foundation models

- **Store the routine keyed to activity and time of day, dated.** The buffer LLM learns only because every sighting carries its day; with weekday-only stamps (our first run) it could not tell which Friday a sighting was from and never learned. Consolidation that keeps a narrative of a typical day loses the sparse windows; consolidation must keep the (object × time-of-day) structure, i.e. be a table of counts, not a story.
- **An instruction is a retrieval cue, and a memory update only for what was kept.** It moved the buffer's cold accuracy by +33.3 in the spell and did not measurably move the table's. It is applied selectively — to the resident it names, not the other one — for exactly as long as it still matches the evidence. It also needs an end: a dated fact that is never retracted keeps being read as the present, and costs a little accuracy everywhere once it is stale.
- **Index by context, not by time.** The regime-tagged counter gets plasticity (it re-learns in the spell as fast as the forgetting counter) at a fifth of the stability cost (return drop −4.7, se 1.3, against −22.9, se 4.3) because the shift partitions the memory instead of decaying it. For an LLM memory that is one column.
- **Confidence should come from the memory's coverage.** The buffer says 0.87 whatever it knows; the tagged counter's confidence is lowest exactly when its bins are empty.
- **Controls that made the result readable**: the cold/warm split (day-level accuracy flatters any recency-driven agent); a calendar that runs the same routine every day, with weekday names removed from the prompt (the LLM was reasoning "it is Saturday, she sleeps in" about a world that keeps no Saturdays); dated timestamps; identical streams for every agent; a prompt leak check.
- **Limitations.** One simulator, one model (Qwen3.8-27B, greedy), one shift type, 10 households; the point is the shape and the controls, which transfer. The consolidated memory is a family of three designs that fail the same way, not a proof that no consolidation can work.
