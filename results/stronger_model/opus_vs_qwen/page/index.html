What the comparison found

Three homes, one run each. Both models read exactly the record the robot built in the original Qwen run. Opus is a fresh Claude call per prompt with no tools; Qwen is plain Qwen3.8, the study's own model.

Opus's notes see both spells; Qwen's see neitherOpus records the change starting and ending on the right night, both times, in all three homes. Qwen's notes record where things were on the latest day and never record any change. A blind reader preferred Opus's notes in all three homes.
Opus forms the hypothesis, then forgets itOn night 32 Opus links the second spell to the first in all three homes. Where it rewrote the note holding the first spell (hh_s2 night 32, hh_s48 night 34) the link is gone within two nights. Where it set the note aside (hh_s32) it kept the link and predicted the return "around day 41"; it came on day 42.
Where they look first barely differsOver the 14 days scored, Opus as chooser is 1.9 points ahead (2 standard errors 1.5). Given Qwen's notes instead of its own, Opus changes its first room on 8 or 9 of 336 questions per home: the choice follows the sightings, not the notes.
Never told about illnessNo prompt mentions illness or health. In the home, only small things change: the ill person's vitamins and glass move to the bedside and the blanket to the bed. Opus names illness as a possibility on night 14 in all three homes, then settles on "leave or a holiday" because the person is up and about.
