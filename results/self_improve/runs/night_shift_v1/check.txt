VERDICT: DO NOT RUN. 6 check(s) failed.
  1. even at its most visible hour the change shows on only 20% of the objects that moved - a robot cannot learn what it cannot see
  2. THE SCENARIO HAS NO BREAK IN IT: 'a timetable that never forgets' does not lose anything at all - it GAINS 0.1 points on the first 2 disrupted days (need 8). A counting method that cannot be broken by the upset means there is nothing in the data for a language model to notice. This is a failing scenario, not a failing method
  3. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' only reaches 69% by the end of the settled window, under the 70% needed - the ordinary routine is not learnable here, so nothing can be lost at the upset either
  4. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' gains only +7.7 points from its first two days to the end of the settled window (need +10) - the settled routine is not being learnt
  5. THE SCENARIO HAS NO BREAK IN IT: 'a timetable with a three-day memory' does not lose anything at all - it GAINS 2.5 points on the first 2 disrupted days (need 8). A counting method that cannot be broken by the upset means there is nothing in the data for a language model to notice. This is a failing scenario, not a failing method
  6. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' recovers only +4.0 points across the rest of the upset (need +5) - the upset is a shock rather than a new routine, so there is nothing in it for a memory to learn

Warnings (9) - not disqualifying, but say them in the paper:
  1. 51% of moved objects do not return to where they were - the reversion measurement is mostly measuring the world, not the memory
  2. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' loses 7.6 points at the return while having no break at all, so the fourth leg was not counted: with nothing adopted, a drop at the return measures the disrupted window having been easier to predict, not a memory being lost
  3. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' drops 7.0 points at the return on the objects the upset never touched, against 10.8 on the ones it moved - most of the fourth leg is the return being a harder prediction problem, not a memory losing a routine; do not read it as the latter
  4. re-learn (never forgets) passes with under 2 points to spare, so that leg is decided by noise rather than by the scenario; do not quote it as a margin
  5. 'a timetable that never forgets' break is +0.1 points with a household-clustered error of 3.5, inside two standard errors of nothing; this null only excludes a change larger than about 7 points, it does not show there is none
  6. 'a timetable that never forgets' re-learn is +5.6 points with a household-clustered error of 3.6, inside two standard errors of nothing; this null only excludes a change larger than about 13 points, it does not show there is none
  7. 'a timetable that never forgets' break again is +0.4 points with a household-clustered error of 1.5, inside two standard errors of nothing; this null only excludes a change larger than about 3 points, it does not show there is none
  8. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' break is +2.5 points with a household-clustered error of 3.0, inside two standard errors of nothing; this null only excludes a change larger than about 9 points, it does not show there is none
