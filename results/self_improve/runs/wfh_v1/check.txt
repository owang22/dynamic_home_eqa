VERDICT: DO NOT RUN. 3 check(s) failed.
  1. even at its most visible hour the change shows on only 38% of the objects that moved - a robot cannot learn what it cannot see
  2. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' gains only +5.9 points from its first two days to the end of the settled window (need +10) - the settled routine is not being learnt
  3. THE SCENARIO HAS NO BREAK IN IT: 'a timetable with a three-day memory' loses only 6.1 points on the first 2 disrupted days (need 8). A counting method that cannot be broken by the upset means there is nothing in the data for a language model to notice. This is a failing scenario, not a failing method

Warnings (6) - not disqualifying, but say them in the paper:
  1. 43% of moved objects do not return to where they were - the reversion measurement is mostly measuring the world, not the memory
  2. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' loses 3.7 points at the return while having no break at all, so the fourth leg was not counted: with nothing adopted, a drop at the return measures the disrupted window having been easier to predict, not a memory being lost
  3. break (never forgets), settled level (three-day memory) passes with under 2 points to spare, so that leg is decided by noise rather than by the scenario; do not quote it as a margin
  4. 'a timetable that never forgets' break again is +0.6 points with a household-clustered error of 2.8, inside two standard errors of nothing; this null only excludes a change larger than about 6 points, it does not show there is none
  5. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' break is -6.1 points with a household-clustered error of 4.2, inside two standard errors of nothing; this null only excludes a change larger than about 14 points, it does not show there is none
  6. 'a timetable with a three-day memory' break again is -3.7 points with a household-clustered error of 2.9, inside two standard errors of nothing; this null only excludes a change larger than about 9 points, it does not show there is none
