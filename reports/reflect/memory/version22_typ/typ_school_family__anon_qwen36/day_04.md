# Household memory — after day 4 (5 day(s) observed)

## Persona hypotheses (up to 3, probs sum to 1)
1. (p=0.60) Two-Person Household (Dual Commuters) — Day 4 shows only one departure (object_9) at 08:09, while previous days had two. However, object_13 (previously seen leaving) returned at 17:57, consistent with a commuter. The absence of a second departure on Friday morning suggests either a schedule change (e.g., WFH for one person) or that object_13 is not a person. The simultaneous departures on Days 2-3 strongly implied two people. The single departure on Day 4 weakens this slightly but doesn't rule it out if one person works from home.
2. (p=0.25) Single Resident with Errands + Guest/Partner — If object_9 is the primary resident's item, and object_13/object_11 were guest/partner items, the pattern is inconsistent. Object_13 left on Day 2 but not Day 3 or 4? Wait, Day 4 shows object_13 returning. It didn't leave on Day 4 morning. This suggests object_13 might be a stationary object (like a laptop) that was moved to 'elsewhere' (office?) on Day 2 but not Day 3/4? Or the resident is home. The 'Single Resident' hypothesis struggles with the multiple distinct 'away' objects (9, 11, 13, 10) appearing and disappearing. However, if object_9 is the only person, and objects 10, 11, 13 are items, then the 'departures' are just items being taken out. But object_13 returned at 17:57, which is a typical return time. This hypothesis is weak because it requires complex item-tracking logic.
3. (p=0.15) Shift Worker (Single) — The varying departure times (08:12, 08:14, 08:19, 08:09) and the fact that only one object (object_9) left on Day 4 could suggest a single person with a flexible or shifting schedule. However, the simultaneous departures on Days 2-3 are hard to explain for a single person unless they are carrying multiple items. The probability remains low.
