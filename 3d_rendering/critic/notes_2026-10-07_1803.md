## 1. Residents sleep naked, go to work without underwear, and wear the same underwear every day (A, F)

  `end_of_day.json` shows **nobody wearing anything**. At the end of days 1 and 3 Mara wears nothing.
  The cause is `prompts.BEDTIME`: "Before you sleep ... take off what you have been wearing today."
  The model follows it literally, after it has already changed into sleepwear.
- **Daniel's whole first sick day (day 3) has zero `wear` events.** He woke with nothing on and stayed
  that way until bed. Meanwhile `WOKE_UP` tells him "you are still in what you slept in", and the
  worn line in his prompt reads "nothing you need to keep track of". The prompt contradicts itself.

  down on the bed foot in the same answer** ("Leave the clothing I moved aside"). She leaves for
  work without them (`leave_home` carried list, day 3). The engine accepts `put_down` of something worn
  and does not check what was just put on.
- **One pair, every day.** Mara wears `underwear_blue_cotton_pair`, `bra_beige_cotton`,
  `socks_cream_wool_pair`, `work_blouse_cream_button_front` and `work_trousers_charcoal_straight_leg`

  `work_trousers_navy_utility` out of the laundry hamper and wears them. The household has 2

Why it matters later: clothing makes up **199 of the 627 object actions** on days 1-3 (`share.py`), so it
is the largest group of objects that move. The pattern the memory methods would learn ("Mara's
underwear lives on the bed foot, always the same pair") is an artifact of the prompt and the engine.

trivial, and they are wrong about real life.

How sure: certain about the events. The bedtime-prompt cause is very likely: the 21:49 "why" text
matches the BEDTIME wording.
Smallest check: count nights where `end_of_day.json` has no worn sleepwear, across days 1-7.
Direction: make BEDTIME say "change into what you sleep in". In the engine, reject `put_down` of
something put on in the same answer. Add a clean/dirty flag (worn -> dirty, washer -> clean) and
3-7 days of underwear and socks per person.

## 2. A resident picks up things from closed drawers in their room without knowing they are there (D, C)

`world._reach` never looks at the resident's memory for objects in the same room. If the object is
anywhere in the room, including a closed drawer the resident has never seen it in, `pick_up` succeeds
and walks them straight to it. I tested this (`scratch/reach_test.py`, using the hand-written test
layout). B moves A's glasses from the nightstand into the closet while A is in another room. A comes
in, still believes "nightstand", calls `pick_up glasses_red`, and gets them from the closet. There
is no "not at the nightstand" message and no search.

Why it matters later: (a) searching for a lost object inside a room never happens, so events
like the disturbance "He cannot find his wallet and reconstructs the previous day's route" cannot
play out. (b) The event log shows a direct walk to the right drawer, so any later analysis of
"resident searched and was wrong" will undercount. (c) Beliefs are not logged at all (they exist
only in the pickles), so the size of this cannot be measured from the run files.
How sure: certain for the code path. I do not know how often it fired in this run, because beliefs
are not logged. My event-based check (`leak.py`) has too little power to tell.
Smallest check: log the resident's believed position on every pick-up next to the true source,
and count the mismatches.
Direction: in-room `pick_up` succeeds only if the believed spot is the true spot, or the object can
be seen. Otherwise it goes through `_not_found`.

## 6. "On" next to an object is saved as "on top of" it (B, A)

In `world._put_down` (and `household.start_position`), when the relation is `on` or empty and a
reference object is given, it becomes `on_top_of`. In the day 1-3 events, 13 of 18 relative
placements were converted this way. In at least 4 of them the model's own `why` says *beside*:
"Leave the library book beside the glasses" was saved as `library_book_red_paperback on_top_of
reading_glasses_clear_amber_frames`; "Set the spoon beside the bowl"; "Keep the sauce beside the
pasta"; "Put the badge with the work bag". The kettle is saved as on top of the toaster on days
1 and 2, and on day 2 Mara calls that "its usual place on the toaster".

Why it matters later: the 3D placement step will stack a kettle on a toaster and a book on a pair
of glasses. Questions that use the relation ("what is on top of the glasses?") will be wrong. Once
the model sees the kettle on the toaster, it starts treating that as the kettle's usual place.
How sure: certain about the conversion. The share that is really "beside" is a reading of the `why`
texts.
Smallest check: for each saved `on_top_of`, check that the size of the thing on top is no larger
than the size of the thing below it.
Direction: when a reference is given with `on`, save `next_to`, or ask for the relation explicitly
and reject a missing one.

## 12. Resuming from a checkpoint silently keeps the old system prompt, schemas and schedule (C, E)

`_save_checkpoint` pickles the whole `Household`, including `self.systems` (the resident system
prompts), `self.act_schemas` and `self.schedule`. After `--resume` on newer code, the user prompts
come from the new `prompts.py`, but the system prompt, the action schema and the schedule are the
old ones. No output file records the git commit or that a resume happened (`run.log` is stdout;
`usage.json` has no commit). `Caller` is recreated on resume, so the `calls` and `cost_usd` in
`usage.json` restart from zero. `_cut_logs` trims `events/responses/failed.jsonl` but not
`calls.jsonl`.

Why it matters later: a run that was "fixed and resumed" mixes two prompt versions, and nothing in
the files shows it. Spend for a 10-household run will be undercounted.
How sure: high from reading the code. Not exercised in this run (no resume yet).
Direction: write the commit hash, plus a hash of `RESIDENT_SYSTEM` and the act schema, into
`usage.json` per day. On resume, rebuild `systems`, `act_schemas` and `schedule` from the current
code, or refuse when the hashes differ.
