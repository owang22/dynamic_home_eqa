## 1. positions.json is not enough to place objects: it keeps a spot's centre and area but not its shape, its door, or the floor outline (B)

What each place's `geometry` has: scene, hssd_spot, source, center, height, area, plus the
furniture's template, instance, position, rotation and scale (`hssd_home.geometry()`, line 196).
The extractor works out more than that, and `geometry()` throws it away:
- **The spot's extent.** The extractor has the spot's `points` (the triangles of that level). Only
  the centre and the area reach the file. For the 138 spots that come from an HSSD receptacle mesh,
  a placer can read the mesh again by the name in `source`. **For the 46 spots whose `source` is
  just `"geometry"`, there is no file to read again.** They exist only as the extractor's
  computation. In those 46 are the bedroom_1 nightstand tops and drawers, the toilet-room and
  shower-room sink cabinets, all 10 spots of the two bathroom_2 racks, the curved bathroom shelf,
  every sink basin except the kitchen's, the bathtub and the tall oven cabinet. **40-43 objects sit
  on these 46 spots at each day end, and another 32-37 on the 9 floor spots** (138 spots come from
  receptacle meshes, 46 from "geometry", 9 are floors).
- **Which door hides a spot.** The extractor writes `behind_door` (for example
  `Dresser0171_door02` for `living_room_tv_console_lower_right_cabinet`). `positions.json` does not.
  Its `source` names only the root link ("... on link Dresser0171"). To show or hide a thing in a
  cupboard, a renderer has to open the right door, and nothing in the file says which one. For
  drawers the link name is inside the `source` text. For the four bedroom_1 nightstand drawers
  (`source: "geometry"`) the link (`Nightstand0026_drawer01/02`) is lost too.
- **Floors.** Each room has one floor spot: an interior point and the room's area (36 m2 for the
  living room). There is no polygon and no furniture footprint. On day 2, 15 objects are on
  `bedroom_2_floor` (backpack and contents, shoes, jeans, apron, pajamas, slippers), and 11 are on
  `bedroom_1_floor`. A placer cannot find free floor from the file.
- **Object facts.** `carrier` (none / open / closed / pockets) is not in the object records, so a
  placer cannot tell that `inside tray_shallow_walnut` is a tray and `inside fork_four_tine_steel`
  is a fork (see 4).
- Also dropped: `hssd_filter` (PARTNR's `height_filtered` means "above a robot's 1.2 m reach").
  23-25 objects a day sit on such spots.

Why it matters later: the Habitat placement step either re-runs `hssd_layout.extract()` (another
input, and it can drift from the run if the extractor changes) or guesses. Objects on "geometry"
spots end up piled at one point, and things in cupboards stay behind closed doors.
How sure: certain about what is missing (compare `extract.json` with any `day_XX/positions.json`).
Smallest check: write a 50-line placer that reads only `day_03/positions.json` plus the HSSD scene
files, and count the objects it cannot give a pose to.
Direction: copy `points` (or a 2D outline), `behind_door`, the link, `hssd_filter` and the room
polygons into `positions.json` once per run (they do not change by day), and add `carrier` to each
object.
