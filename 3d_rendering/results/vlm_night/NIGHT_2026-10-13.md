# Night of 2026-10-12/13: asking about the rendered home with a vision model

Oliver asked to spend the night trying new ways to ask questions in the lived-home setting, now that
snapshots can be rendered in 3D, with a local vision model. Everything here is household A
(Mara and Julian, HSSD scene 107734254_176000121), days 4-14, no paid API calls.

An independent auditor checked the first draft against the files (`AUDIT_2026-10-13.md`). It found
six real problems; all are fixed or stated below, and the search runs were redone after the fixes.
The pre-audit runs are kept in `questions/old_v1/pre_audit/`.

## Setup

- **Model:** Qwen3.8-27B with its own vision tower, served by vLLM on port 8300 (local, free).
  - A 640x480 picture costs about 300 prompt tokens.
  - Temperature 0, thinking off unless stated.
  - Every yes/no or pick-one answer is forced by the output grammar (see "Faults found").
  - Answers are cached on disk, keyed by the picture file's time stamp.
- **Pictures** (`render/`, made by a helper agent; see `render/README.md`):
  - One fixed robot viewpoint per open spot: 92 spots (tables, counters, seats, shelves, floors, sinks), camera at 1.0 m, 75° lens.
  - All 112 snapshots rendered (14 days × 8 times), 20 s each.
  - Every picture comes with the pixel count of every object drawn in it.
  - Pictures: `/mnt/nvme/oliver/robot/render_methods/vlm_night/hssd_a/`.
  - A second set at 1.4 m: question times, days 4-14 (`hssd_a_1p4/`, `render/README_1p4.md`).
- **Ground truth rules:**
  - Patrol notes: a thing is noted for a piece of furniture only if it is placed on that furniture and covers at least 300 px in its picture. The helper's eye check found things under ~300 px cannot be named.
  - A where-is question is asked only when the thing covers at least 500 px at question time.
  - A look counts as finding the thing if the thing is drawn in that furniture's pictures at 300 px or more, whatever it rests on. (The first draft counted only things placed on that furniture, so a correct sighting of the thing on a neighbouring table counted as a false find. Fixed.)
- **Code:** `questions/`
  - `vlm.py`: client; `yes_no` and `choice` force the answer format.
  - `world.py`: snapshots, views, truth.
  - `search.py`: where-is with looks.
  - `kinds.py`: other question kinds.
  - `judge_bench.py`: the look step alone.
  - `presence_wording.py`: wording variants.
  - `noise_rerun.py`: the same arms asked again with an empty cache.

## 1. Where is it? Search with looks (174 questions)

**The task:**
- The robot patrols a third of the rooms at 08, 12, 16 and 20 h. At 10, 14, 18 and 22 h it is asked "Where is the <thing>?".
- It may look at up to 8 pieces of furniture (77 have views). Each look shows that furniture's pictures at question time.
- Things are named by type, and only types with at most 2 objects in the house are asked; any object of the type counts. At most 2 questions per type per category.
- **Categories, by the perfect patrol notes:**
  - 77 moved since last seen (44 types)
  - 60 at last sighting (40 types)
  - 37 never seen (24 types)

**Three parts are varied separately:**
- the order of looks (a rule, or the model reading the notes);
- the patrol notes (perfect, or the model's own list of what it sees in each patrol picture, or the raw pictures);
- the look judge (perfect, or the model shown the pictures).

The model's ranking sees 8 days of notes before the question time (the code says 7; the window
includes both ends).

Found within 8 looks, all 174 questions (moved since last seen in brackets):

| order of looks | notes | look judged by | found | false finds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nearest first | none | perfect | 10% (10%) | 0 |
| last seen | perfect | perfect | 45% (22%) | 0 |
| most often seen | perfect | perfect | 61% (57%) | 0 |
| **model ranks 8 from the notes** | perfect | perfect | **91% (84%)** | 0 |
| model | model's own picture lists | perfect | 84% (87%) | 0 |
| model | 40 raw recent patrol pictures | perfect | 53% (48%) | 0 |
| model | perfect | model, asked by name | 51% (48%) | 47 |
| model | perfect | model, by name, "could anything be…" | 56% (42%) | 52 |
| model | perfect | model, shown an **oracle crop** of the thing | 61% (60%) | 31 |
| model | model's own lists | model, by name | 46% (49%) | 56 |
| model | model's own lists | model, "could anything be…" | 48% (44%) | 66 |
| model | model's own lists | model, oracle crop | 55% (62%) | 46 |
| model, thinking on | perfect | perfect | 92% (84%) | 0 |
| model, thinking on | model's own lists | perfect | 84% (84%) | 0 |

"Oracle crop" means a close-up of the thing cut from an earlier patrol picture using the
renderer's true object mask. A robot would not have that mask, so this stands for "someone showed
the robot a photo of the thing", not for something the robot can do alone.

**Paired counts** (questions only the first arm finds vs only the second; exact test):

| comparison | found | only first / only second | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| model ranking vs most often seen (perfect notes and looks) | 159 vs 106 | 55 / 2 | < 0.001 |
| same, moved since only | 65 vs 44 | 23 / 2 | < 0.001 |
| perfect notes vs model's own lists (model ranking) | 159 vs 146 | 18 / 5 | 0.01 |
| 40 raw pictures vs most often seen | 93 vs 106 | 27 / 40 | 0.14 |
| look by oracle crop vs by name (perfect notes) | 107 vs 89 | 28 / 10 | 0.005 |
| look by oracle crop vs "could anything be…" | 107 vs 97 | 26 / 16 | 0.16 |
| false finds, oracle crop vs "could anything be…" | 31 vs 52 | 10 / 31 | 0.001 |
| false finds, oracle crop vs by name | 31 vs 47 | 10 / 26 | 0.01 |
| "could anything be…" vs by name | 97 vs 89 | 18 / 10 | 0.19 |

**What this says:**
- **Planning is not the problem; seeing is.**
  - With perfect looks, the model's own ranking from the notes finds 91%, against 61% for "most often seen" (55 vs 2).
  - On things that moved since last seen it finds 84% against 57%.
  - With the model's own eyes at each look, the same ranking finds 51%. The 40-point gap is the look step.
  - Even when the thing is still where it was last seen, the model by name finds it in 34 of 60 questions; with perfect looks 58 of those 60 are found at the first look.
- **Writing patrol notes from pictures costs about 7 points** (91% → 84%; 18 vs 5).
  - The loss is all on things that had not moved (60/60 → 48/60): the model's lists use looser names ("cable" for a phone charger, "shirt" for a hoodie) and miss small things.
  - On moved things it is level (65 vs 67). With vaguer notes the model falls back on common sense (sketch pad → office desk, loafers → entryway), which sometimes wins.
- **Keeping 40 raw pictures instead of notes is no better than "most often seen"** (53% vs 61%, p = 0.14). The 40 pictures reach back 2 patrols for 130 questions and part of a third for 44.
- **The look judge:**
  - Asking by name and asking "could anything be the <type>, even if it looks different?" find about the same (89 vs 97, p = 0.19).
  - The oracle crop finds 10 more than the loose wording (not a claim at this n), but makes far fewer wrong yeses: 31 false finds against 52.
  - So what a photo of the thing buys is mostly fewer wrong yeses, not more finds.
  - Wrong yeses end the search early. Of the 47 false finds by name, 21 came after the robot had already looked at the right furniture and said no.

## 2. The look step alone (`judge_bench.py`)

Items: the right furniture (174), the old spot of a moved thing (77), and other furniture with
things on it (100). Rows that skip items say so.

| how the model is asked | says yes at the right spot | at the old spot | elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|
| by name, "Is a <type> visible?" | 105/174 (60%) | 18/77 | 10/100 |
| by name, "could any object be a <type>, even if it looks a bit different or is partly hidden?" | 126/174 (72%) | 20/77 | 15/100 |
| oracle crop, "is this same thing here?" | 118/153 | 13/76 | 7/88 |
| by name, camera at 1.4 m | 104/150 | 13/77 | 12/97 |
| by name, thinking on | 107/167 (7 unparsed) | 16/77 | 12/100 |

**Crop vs wording, on the same items:**
- Right spot, crop from other furniture (a real change of view), 111 items: crop 80, "could anything be" 81, by name 67. Crop vs "could anything be": 17 vs 18. **No difference in finding.**
- Right spot, crop from the same furniture, 42 items: crop 38. This is close to matching a picture against itself, so it says little.
- Old spot (76 items): crop says yes 13, "could anything be" 20 (3 vs 10, p = 0.09).
- Elsewhere (88 items): crop 7, "could anything be" 13 (2 vs 8, p = 0.11).
- The first draft said the crop "finds more" (80 vs 67 by name, p = 0.04). That was the change of wording, not the crop: the crop prompt also says "it may be seen from another side or partly hidden".
- In 11 right-spot items the crop shows the other object of the same type (the steel thermos when the red one is there). The model's "no" is then correct but scored as a miss.

**Camera height: no measurable difference.**
- Overall 104/150 at 1.4 m vs 100/150 at 1.0 m (23 vs 19).
- The first draft split counters from floors after seeing the results (counters 21 vs 12, p = 0.16) and called it suggestive. That split was chosen after the fact and does not pass the bar.
- The draft's "counters gain 35% more pixels" also does not hold for the items asked: their median pixel ratio (1.4 m / 1.0 m) is 0.96.

**Recognition by size** (single pictures, the spot named, "Is there a <type> on it?"):
- Absent types: yes 15/300.
- 165 of the 600 "there" items were on another spot of the same furniture, not the spot named. The model's "no" was then right. On the named spot only, it says yes 248/435 (57%):

  | thing's size in the picture | says yes when it is on the named spot |
  |---|---|
  | 30-500 px | 15/62 |
  | 500-3000 px | 102/186 |
  | 3000+ px | 131/187 |

- Above 500 px: things whose model is right say yes 175/268 (65%); near stand-ins 58/105 (55%).
- The model is cautious, not fooled. Asking it to list the objects first made it worse (194/600).

## 3. Other ways to ask (`kinds.py`)

| question | right | chance | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What changed:** two pictures of one spot 2 h apart, "which of these was put on / taken off?" | 167/240 (70%) | 24% | added 59/99, removed 68/101, no change 40/40; "nothing" was also picked 13 times when something had changed |
| **Usual place:** "The <thing> is usually kept on X. Is it there now?" (one picture of X) | 239/300 (80%) | 50% | yes when there 121/150, yes when not 32/150 |
| **Stray, from the picture:** "which of these was left here and is normally kept elsewhere?" | 73/150 (49%) | 38% | the household's own usual places as the key |
| **Stray, given the usual places as text** | 150/150 | 38% | a check, trivially readable |

- **What changed** is the strongest new kind: well above chance, and the two-picture form needs no names to be exact.
  - "No change 40/40" may partly be a habit: the model also said "nothing" 13 times when something had changed.
  - 49 of the 200 changed items have the same spot-vs-furniture issue as the presence test, so the true score is likely a little higher.
- **Stray** is about at chance from the picture.
  - The 150 questions come from only 11 spots (27 from one counter), repeating over consecutive times, so the real sample is far smaller than 150.
  - The key is this household's arrangement: most strays are kitchen things moved within the kitchen. The model got pasta (16/16) and water bottles (12/14) right.
  - It also picks things a visitor would call out of place: reading glasses 15 times and wine glasses 13 times, never right.
  - So a stray question tests knowledge of the residents, not sight. That makes it a natural place for memory to help.

## 4. Thinking on

Thinking changes nothing measurable, and costs about 4 minutes per answer instead of a few seconds.

- **Ranking the looks** (perfect looks):
  - Perfect notes: 160 vs 159 without thinking (4 vs 3 paired).
  - Own notes: 146 vs 146 (9 vs 9).
  - Found at the first look it is slightly worse: 83 vs 87, and 69 vs 77.
  - Median 260 s per ranking. With 8000 tokens, 42 of 174 ran out and gave no list; with 30000, 0 of 174 (perfect notes) and 2 of 174 (own notes).
- **The look step** (by name, same items as the plain run): right spot 107 vs 104 of 167 (13 vs 10), old spot 16 vs 18, elsewhere 12 vs 10. 7 thinking answers at the right spot did not end in an answer line.
- An earlier smoke test suggested thinking makes the model more cautious. It also had the parse fault, and this run does not show it.

## Faults found tonight, and fixed

- **Answer parsing.** The first look judge read the first word of a free reply capped at 5 tokens.
  - The model often began "Based on a careful examination…", which was read as "no" in 49 of 60 looks at the right furniture.
  - Every judged result was redone with the answer forced to yes/no (or a letter) by the output grammar. The broken results are kept in `questions/old_v1/parse_bug/`.
- **A different prompt in the search loop than on the bench.** The loop's by-name prompt still ended "Answer yes or no.", which made the model say yes less often (62/155 vs 95/155 on the bench at the right furniture). Fixed. Together with the scoring fix below, the by-name loop went from 33% to 51%; the two fixes were made at once, so the split between them is not measured.
- **Wrong owner of a pixel.** At first the truth counted any object in a picture, so a chair seat "had" the table's bowls. Notes now count things only for their own furniture; looks count what is visible (see Setup).
- **Renderer** (fixed by the helper before the final set): things meant for a lower shelf were drawn on top of the furniture (office desk shelf onto the desk top, keys onto the piano top). Everything was rendered again.
- **Crop leak in the search loop.** The first crop judge took crops from any snapshot, including times and rooms the robot never patrolled. Now crops come only from patrol pictures.
- **Thinking ran out of room.** 42 of 174 thinking rankings used all 8000 tokens and returned no list, so the search fell back to "nearest first". Those were asked again with 30000 tokens; then all 174 finished with perfect notes, and 172 of 174 with the model's own notes.

## Caveats

- One household, one seed of patrol rooms, 174 search questions: differences under about 10 points are not claims. Paired counts are given where it matters.
- **Rerun noise** (`noise_rerun.py`: the same arms asked again with an empty cache; vLLM at temperature 0 is not exactly repeatable):
  - The model's ranking changed for 22 of 174 questions. With perfect looks, no result changed (159 → 159).
  - Looks by name: 8 results changed, found 89 → 92, false finds 47 → 42.
  - Oracle crop: 7 results changed, totals the same (107, 31).
  - So a difference of up to about 5 questions between model-judged arms can come from rerunning alone.
- Questions name things by type and accept any object of that type. Look-alikes of the same type count as found.
- 4 of the 77 "moved since" questions still have an accepted object at the old spot (ids 35, 119, 126, 141): the old spot is left out of the answer only at 500 px or more, while notes use 300 px. 2 of the 18 "wrong yeses at the old spot" on the bench are in fact right.
- About a third of the things asked are near stand-in models (a crisp packet for pasta, red All Stars for every shoe). By name at the right furniture the model says yes 76/118 for right models and 29/56 for stand-ins.
  - Some misses are the asset or the placement, not the model: the wooden tray is drawn floating 0.3 m above the dining table on the vase; market herbs are a white crumpled shape; the project folder is a black box on edge; the dish towel looks like a paper-towel roll.
  - The verdict file wrongly marks the parcel (a wooden crate) and the cloth napkin (a gold board) as right.
  - One sleep shirt is pure white on an over-bright white floor (1 of 174 items).
- People are not drawn: nothing held, worn or pocketed is asked about.
- The perfect-notes arm is perfect recognition with the camera's real visibility, not perfect knowledge.
- `kinds.py` and the search judges turn an unparsed answer into "no". The auditor scanned the cache: every forced answer parsed, so no result is affected.
- `kinds.py build` picks "what changed" items from Python sets, so a rebuild gives 39 of 240 different items. The stored questions are consistent with the truth.

## Next

   - store a crop of each thing whenever a patrol picture shows it clearly (needs a detector or the model's own boxes, not the true mask), and ask "is this the same thing?" — the oracle-crop result says this mainly cuts wrong yeses;
   - ask "could anything be…" only as a second pass at the top two ranked places;
   - re-plan after a "no" and allow a second look at the top place.
2. Add "what changed" and "is it in its usual place" to the play task. Both are cheap, well above chance, and need no exact names.
3. Fix the assets and placements the model cannot be expected to see (wooden tray, herbs, project folder, dish towel, paper towels, parcel, napkin) and the 4 mislabelled questions; score presence and "what changed" per furniture.
4. Household B, and a second patrol seed, before trusting any difference under 10 points.
5. Camera height: untested in any useful sense. If it matters, choose the counter/floor split in advance and ask more counter items.
