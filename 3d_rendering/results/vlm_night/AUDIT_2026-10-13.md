# Audit of NIGHT_2026-10-13.md (vision-model questions, household A)

Independent check, 2026-10-13 02:51. I changed no files except this one. My scripts are in my
scratch folder. They recompute everything from `questions/search/*.json`, `judge/*.json`,
`kinds/*.json` and the `truth.json` / `*_ids.png` files.

## Confirmed problems, most serious first

1. **The crop-versus-name result in the search loop mixes the crop with a wording change.**
   - In the loop, the by-name look (`search.py judge_vlm`) ends its prompt with "Answer yes or no."
   - The crop look (`judge_ref`) and the bench prompts (`judge_bench.py`) do not have that ending.
   - On the same question, furniture and pictures, the loop's by-name judge and the bench's "strict"
     judge disagree on 44 of 256 looks. All 44 go one way: the bench says yes, the loop says no.
   - At the right furniture the loop's by-name judge says yes on 62/155. The bench says yes on 95/155
     of the same looks.
   - So the headline "crop raises the full loop from 33% to 51% (paired 35 vs 3)" compares a crop
     prompt against a by-name prompt that is stricter just because of its wording. Part of the gain,
     possibly most of it, is the wording.
   - Section 1's "says yes 60% of the time when asked by name (105/174)" is the bench figure. The
     loop's own by-name judge ran at about 40%.

2. **On the bench, the crop's gain at the right spot also matches a wording cue.**
   - The crop prompt adds "It may be seen from another side or partly hidden."
   - The "lenient" by-name prompt carries the same cue: "could be … even if it looks different or
     is partly hidden".
   - On the 111 items whose crop came from different furniture:

     | comparison | at the right spot | at the old spot | elsewhere |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | crop vs strict name | 80 vs 67 (paired 24 vs 11) | | |
     | crop vs lenient name | 80 vs 81 (paired 17 vs 18) | 12 vs 18 (paired 2 vs 8) | 7 vs 13 (2 vs 8) |

   - So the crop does not find more than the lenient name wording. What it does is say yes less often
     where the thing is not.
   - "Crop vs name: crop finds 80 against 67 (p = 0.04)" should say instead: the crop cuts wrong
     yeses, and finds about as many as the lenient name wording.

3. **The crop uses information the robot could not have.**
   - `reference_crop` picks the asked object out of an earlier picture using the renderer's object-id
     mask (`*_ids.png`). It chooses the largest view by true pixel count and cuts a tight box, 12 px
     around the true mask.
   - Limiting crops to patrol pictures (the "crop leak" fix) limits *when* the crop was taken. It does
     not remove the oracle part: the robot would have to know already which pixels are "the sleep
     shirt".
   - This matters most in the "model's own lists + crop" row: the notes come from the model, but the
     crop is perfect.
   - All crop rows should be labelled "oracle crop (true mask)". Otherwise the comparison should be
     framed as "a person shows a photo".

4. **A different scoring rule creates false finds.**
   - The look prompt asks "Is a <type> visible in any of these pictures?". Scoring counts only things
     placed *on that furniture*.
   - When the camera at one piece of furniture shows the thing sitting on a neighbouring one, the
     model's correct "yes" is scored as a false find, and the search stops there.
   - Share of false finds where the thing really is visible in the pictures:

     | search arm | false finds where the thing is visible |
     |---|---|
     | perfect notes + crop | 11 of 35 |
     | own lists + crop | 13 of 52 |
     | by name | 4 of 32 |

   - Example, checked by eye: question 81, "backpack", day 8 10:00. Looking at the kitchen floor, the
     bright backpack is plainly on the island in the picture. The model said yes and it was scored as
     a false find.
   - This lowers the crop arms most. It also makes the "false finds" column overstate errors.

5. **The presence test ("recognition by size", 306/600) names a spot, but the truth is per furniture.**
   - `World.truth()` assigns a thing to every spot of its furniture. The presence prompt says "This
     picture shows the <spot>. Is there a <type> on it?".
   - In 165 of the 600 "there" items the thing sits on a *different spot* of the same furniture.
     Examples: herbs on the counter beside the sink, asked about the sink basin; a saucepan on the
     right cooktop, asked about the left cooktop (I looked at this picture: day 12 16:00, left
     cooktop).
   - On those 165 items the model said yes 58 times. On the 435 items where the thing is on the named
     spot it said yes 248 times (57%, not 51%).
   - Bands, items on the named spot only:

     | thing's size | says yes |
     |---|---|
     | 30-500 px | 15/62 |
     | 500-3000 px | 102/186 |
     | 3000+ px | 131/187 (70%) |

   - The "cautious" reading still holds, but the curve is pulled down by questions where "no" is
     arguably correct.
   - The same mismatch touches "what changed": in 49 of 200 items the answer object sits on another
     spot of the furniture (27 right).

6. **The camera-height claim is overstated, and its supporting pixel numbers do not hold for these
   items.**
   - 21 vs 12 (p = 0.16) is below the project's own bar of two standard errors (z about 1.6).
   - Floors and low tables go the other way (2 vs 7).
   - Overall, on the same 150 items, 1.4 m says yes 104 times and 1.0 m 100 times: no difference.
   - The split at 0.7 m was made after the fact.
   - "The helper's pixel counts agree: counters gain about 35%" is the helper's median over all
     objects. For the 91 counter items actually asked, the asked thing's median pixel ratio
     (1.4 m / 1.0 m) is 0.96, and the summed ratio is 0.84. Over all owned objects on counters at
     question times, the total is unchanged (ratio 1.00; the median per object is 1.22).
   - So the pixels do not explain the gain. "Suggestive" is too strong. Next step 2 (move the mast)
     rests on it.
   - The 1.4 m set has no office-desk-shelf picture, so 24 office-desk items are dropped. The table
     row 104/150 is not on the same items as 105/174.

## Smaller problems

- **Mismatches in the numbers:**
  - "nothing was picked 12 times when something had changed": it is **13**.
  - "That gives 44-48 types per category": the types are **44 / 40 / 24** (moved / at last sighting /
    never seen).
  - "40 raw pictures cover only the last one or two patrols": they reach 2 patrols for 130 questions
    and part of a third for 44.
- **The "thinking on" row says "see addendum".** There is no addendum, `judge/run_think.log` is
  empty, and there is no `judge/think.json`.
- **The bench table compares different item sets.**
  - The crop row 118/153 includes the 42 same-spot crops (38/42, close to matching a picture against
    itself), so its 77% is inflated. On the 111 crops from different furniture it is 80/111 (72%).
  - The old-spot (76 vs 77) and elsewhere (88 vs 100) denominators also differ between rows.
- **The crop can show the other object of the same type.**
  - Types with 2 objects accept either object, but the crop is "this exact <type>".
  - In 11 bench "there" items the crop shows the twin, for example the steel thermos when the red
    one is there. The model's "no" is then correct but scored as a miss (crop yes 5/11, name yes 9/11).
  - This works against the crop.
- **4 of the 77 "moved since" questions are mislabelled.** An accepted object is still placed at the
  old spot: ids 35, 119, 126 and 141 (reading glasses, sleep shirt, sleep pants).
  - In two of them the glasses show at 342 and 402 px.
  - Of the 18 "wrong yes at the old spot", 2 are correct. I checked by eye: question 119, day 10
    14:00, bathroom-2 floor, where the bronze reading glasses are plainly on the floor.
  - The cause: the old spot is excluded from `where` only at 500 px or more, and the 300 px note
    threshold differs from the 500 px question threshold.
- **Several "the model fails to see it" cases are asset or render problems, not the model:**
  - "Wooden tray" (questions 89, 99, 114): on the dining table it is drawn as a dark ribbed basket
    resting on top of the table's centrepiece vase, about 0.3 m above the table top (day 9 22:00).
    That is a placement fault.
  - "Market herbs" is a white crumpled shape; a person could not name it (day 14 18:00, counter by
    the range).
  - "Project folder" on the island is a large black box standing on edge (day 6 18:00).
  - "Dish towel" looks like a paper-towel roll. "Paper towels" is a dark disc hanging at the top of
    the sink picture, which may be another placement fault.
  - The verdict file marks "parcel" (a wooden crate) and "cloth napkin" (a gold board) as "right".
  - At the right furniture, by name, the model says yes on 76/118 for "right" models and 29/56 for
    "near" models. The write-up's "about a quarter" is about a third of the search "there" items.
- **One thing counted as visible was pure white.** The sleep shirt on the bedroom-1 floor (day 4
  18:00) has 2238 px in the truth, but all of them are pure white on an over-bright white floor; it
  cannot be seen. This is rare: 1 of 174 "there" items and 2 of 600 presence items.
- **The "absent" type is sometimes drawn.** In 1 of 15 "yes when absent" presence answers the type
  is in the picture on other furniture (paperback book, toilet floor). In 2 of 18 old-spot yeses the
  thing is there (see above).
- **"No change 40/40" may partly be a habit of answering "nothing".** The model also chose
  "nothing" 13 times when something had changed.
- **The stray test is clustered.** Its 150 questions come from only 11 spots: 27 from one counter,
  and most of the rest repeat arrangements over consecutive times. The real sample is much smaller
  than 150.
  - Of the model's picks, "reading glasses" (15) and "wine glass" (13) were never right. "Pasta"
    (16/16) and "water bottle" (12/14) were mostly right; the write-up leaves these out.
- **`kinds.py` would turn a failed answer into "no".** It wraps `yes_no` in `bool()` (the search
  judges do too), so an answer that fails to parse counts as "no". I scanned the whole cache: every
  forced-format answer parsed (6758 yes/no, 525 letters, 528 orders, 900 list answers). The failure
  does not occur in these results, but the code would hide it.
- **`kinds.py build` cannot reproduce the "what changed" questions.** It picks from Python sets,
  whose order changes between runs. Rebuilding gave 39 of 240 different change items. The stored
  questions are consistent with the current truth (0 inconsistencies), so the results stand. The
  search, stray, usual and presence questions rebuild identically.
- **No rerun noise floor was measured.** Every comparison uses one cached answer per call.
- **The model is shown 8 days of notes, not 7.** The note window is `day >= question day - 7`.
