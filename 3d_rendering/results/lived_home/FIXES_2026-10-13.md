# Fixes to the lived-in home generator and its search task, 2026-10-13

No model was called. Every number below comes from the two existing 14-day runs
(`hssd_a`, `hssd_b`; 112 snapshots each, 08:00 to 22:00 every 2 hours), read by
offline code. Nothing is committed.

## How "after" was measured: replaying the runs

`lived_home/replay_rules.py` re-applies every recorded action of a run (events.jsonl,
in the order the run applied them) to a fresh `World`, with no model. The residents'
choices stay fixed, so this measures the rules, not how residents would react to
them.

- **Check that it can reproduce the runs:** with the new rules off, the replay
  rebuilds every position in every snapshot exactly: A 28,112 of 28,112, B 30,464 of
  30,464.
- **With the rules on:**
  - 70 put-downs in A and 52 in B are redirected.
  - 53 of A's 7,801 actions then fail (B: 0). Nearly all are later pick-ups of the
    house keys and a mug. These had been dropped on the entryway and hall floors,
    which have no other furniture, and now go to the nearest counter. The scripted
    pick-up looks on the floor and misses them; a live resident would know where
    they put them.
  - When a redirect lands on a full spot, the replay cannot ask the resident again.
    It carries out the rules' choice over the room limit and counts it: 20 in A,
    2 in B.

## 1. Only some things hold others

**Rule:** `lived_home/placement.py`, `holding_problem(item, base, rel)`.
- It uses the existing fields (`type`, `size`, `carrier`, `pocketable`) plus a
  table `BASE_ROLES`.
- **On top of:**
  - a tray, board, plate or coaster (a coaster takes dishes only);
  - a book, notebook or folder, which takes paper, pens, glasses or a phone;
  - a pile of clothes or towels;
  - a box, tin or kit, which takes small things;
  - a bowl, which takes food, cutlery and smaller bowls or plates;
  - a laptop stand or a toothbrush holder.
- **Inside:**
  - a bag, which takes anything that fits;
  - a folder (paper), pencil case (pens), first-aid kit (medicine), wallet (cards,
    receipts, keys), knife roll (knives), food box (food);
  - a mug or glass, which takes pens, cutlery, a toothbrush or a tea bag;
  - a laundry basket or laundry bag (clothes);
  - pockets, which take pocketable things.
- A package of its own contents holds nothing, whatever its `carrier` says. This
  covers yogurt, eggs, a milk carton, a tea tin, tissues, painkillers and a water
  bottle.
- What goes on or in a thing must not be bigger than it.

**Enforcement:** in `World._put_down`.
- A put-down that breaks the rule is carried out *next to* the thing named, at the
  place where it sits (or where the outermost bag around it sits).
- The event keeps `relation_as_said`, `ref_as_said` and the reason (`redirected`).
- If the spot next to it is full, the resident is told the thing named cannot hold
  it and there is no room next to it.
- `world.rules = {"holding": False, "places": False}` restores the old behaviour.

**Start positions:** `household.fix_start_placement` applies the same rules to a new
household and to a reused one (`--household`, but not on `--resume`).
- A: 6 things moved.
- B: 8 things moved, including six eggs that started inside the milk carton.

**Prompts:** two sentences added: the resident action list, and the object-listing
rules at creation.

**Checker:** `python -m lived_home.check_placement <run>`.

| Things on or in another thing that break the rule | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Before (the runs as they are) | 304 (2.7 per snapshot) | 798 (7.1 per snapshot) |
| After (replay, rules on, starts fixed) | 0 | 0 |

- **A before**, largest: metronome on music notebook 56, project folder on laptop 52,
  sketchbook on mug 32, canned tomatoes on carrots 25, earplugs on wallet 21, butter
  on saucepan 14, wooden tray on mixing bowl 4.
- **B before**, largest: egg inside milk carton 582, cocktail napkin on hot sauce 110.
- **Allowed, as intended:** pills, plasters and the thermometer in the first-aid kit;
  mail in the ceramic tray; knives on the cutting board; the dish towel on the wooden

## 3. Floors and basins

**Rule:** `placement.place_problem(item, place, rel, ref)`, with allowed categories
for each kind of place.
- **Basin** (sink, bathtub): dishes, cutlery, toiletries and cleaning things. A
  kitchen sink also takes food.
- **Floor**, or under a piece of furniture: shoes, bags, baskets, boxes, floor
  things, clothes and towels (already counted separately as lying about), and large
  things that are not food, dishes or electronics. A shower or bath floor also takes
  toiletries.
- **Under a thing** (a coaster under a mug) is not the floor.

**Enforcement:** same place as fix 1. The thing goes to a surface of the same
furniture, else the nearest table, counter or shelf in the room. If the room has
none (A's entryway and hall have only a floor), it goes to the nearest one in any
room.

**Bug fixed on the way:** "shelf beneath the kitchen sink" was read as the basin
(`world._spot_words`/`_drop_kind` did not cut at "beneath"). That made the laundry
soap kept there look like it was in the sink.

## 4. The search task

### (a) Answers from memory

**Before:** the simple methods (`play_baselines.py`) answered "can't tell" whenever a
look did not show the thing, so a thing put away or carried was never right.

**Now:** when the thing has not been found, "last seen" and "most often" answer from
their patrol notes:
- **someone has it**, when the last patrol sighting was in a resident's hands;
- **put away**, when it was last seen on a piece of furniture: in that furniture's
  drawers or cupboards, or else in the nearest furniture of the same room that has
  some;
- otherwise **can't tell**.

`--no-memory-answers` gives the old behaviour.

Answers are judged as the play page judges a person (`play_cli.Game.judge`), as
right, partly right or wrong.

### (b) Every question

**Before:** the summary was "found X of the visible ones": 22 of the 30 sampled
questions.

**Now:**
- Every one of the 30 is scored.
- `play.build_all` builds every question the task could ask. That is each askable
  thing at each question time from day 4 on, with the same fairness rules. It uses
  the same patrols as the session, from the same seed.
- This gives 4,755 questions in A and 4,984 in B.
- Run it with `python -m lived_home.play_baselines <run>`.

Right / partly right / wrong:

| | A, 30 questions | A, all 4,755 | B, 30 questions | B, all 4,984 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nearest (no memory, unchanged) | 2 / 28 / 0 | 623 / 4132 / 0 | 2 / 28 / 0 | 378 / 4606 / 0 |
| last seen, before | 9 / 21 / 0 | 3821 / 934 / 0 | 12 / 18 / 0 | 3707 / 1277 / 0 |
| last seen, after | 9 / 8 / 13 | 3827 / 338 / 590 | 14 / 4 / 12 | 3798 / 475 / 711 |
| most often, before | 13 / 17 / 0 | 3997 / 758 / 0 | 17 / 13 / 0 | 3836 / 1148 / 0 |
| most often, after | 13 / 8 / 9 | 4003 / 338 / 414 | 19 / 4 / 7 | 3927 / 474 / 583 |

What this shows:
- **Put away is now sometimes right.** A goes from 0 to 6 of 276; B from 0 to 91 of
  746. Another 165 (A) and 335 (B) get the right room.
- **The cost is in the other column.** Most memory answers replace a "can't tell"
  (partly right) with a wrong one. 417 in A and 326 in B are about things that were
  visible somewhere the 8 looks did not reach.
- **Someone has it is not helped (A 9 of 25, B 19 of 53, all from looks).** The
  patrols see a thing in someone's hands only 9 times in A and 17 in B over 14 days,
  so the notes almost never support the answer.
- **Out of the house stays at 0** (175 in A, 111 in B). Nothing in the notes says a
  person left.
- **Most questions are easy.** Over all questions, most are about a thing still
  where a patrol last saw it (3,740 of 4,755 in A), which is why every question gives
  80% against 30% on the 30. The 30 are sampled 40% "moved since last seen".

room). The old spot-level table is `--truth`. The output goes to
`<run>/baselines_notes.md`.

## 5. Design only, not built: laundry and food being used up

### Laundry

The world already has:
- `dirty` (clothes worn on a day are dirty that night);
- `_wash_at` (time spent at a washing machine cleans what is inside it);
- laundry baskets and hampers as places things pass through.

What is missing is the cycle **worn → basket → machine → wet → dry → back to
storage**, and anything that moves residents along it.

1. **States.** Add a `wet` set next to `dirty`.
   - `_wash_at` moves washed things from dirty to wet.
   - A wet thing cannot be worn: `wear` refuses it with "it is still wet".
   - A wet thing on a drying rack, clothes rail or radiator, or inside a dryer drum
     (by spot words, as `_wash_at` finds the washer), is dry after 4 hours, or at
     once after a `do` at the dryer.
   - Wet things anywhere else stay wet, and the views say so.
2. **Labels in the views.** "(dirty)", "(wet)" and "(clean, usually kept in X)",
   using the existing `kept_notes` mechanism. A basket holding 8 or more dirty
   things shows "(laundry basket full)".
   - No instruction to do laundry.
   - A weekly "laundry day" situation in `director.py` (about weekly, for one
     resident) makes a planned activity likely.
3. **Checker** (`check_laundry`), per clothing item per day:
   - its stage;
   - days from dirty to clean;
   - clothes worn again while dirty;
   - clean clothes left lying about for more than a day.
   - **Pass bar:** every dirty item reaches clean within 7 days in a 14-day run.
4. **Positions** gain `"wet": bool`, next to `"dirty"`. The renderer can ignore it.

### Food being used up

1. **Servings.** Add `servings` to food objects at creation, from a small table by
   type (eggs 6, milk 8, bread 10, an apple 1, pasta 5, butter 10), with a model
   field later.
2. **A new verb `use`** (object, amount, default 1) in `schemas.VERBS` and
   `World.apply`. It lowers `servings`. At 0 the thing is used up: its position
   becomes `{"at": "gone", "why": "used up"}`, it is left out of views and positions,
   and its packaging may go to a bin as a new object.
   - `do` stays as it is, so old runs replay unchanged.
3. **Restocking.** When a resident comes home from an activity whose text mentions
   shopping or groceries, the engine adds fresh copies (new id suffix, full
   servings) of the food types that ran out or are under a quarter, into a grocery
   bag in their hands. The resident then puts them away as usual.
4. **Effects on the task.**
   - `answer_key` gains the kind "used up".
   - Questions skip things used up before the question, or ask about them with
     "used up" as an accepted answer.
   - `play.askable` must treat a restocked copy as a different thing.
5. **Checker:** food servings in the home per day stay between a floor and a
   ceiling (no endless fridge, no empty one for more than 2 days), and every
   restock is logged.
6. **Order:** build laundry first. It uses existing pieces only, and does not change
   what the task's answer key can say.
